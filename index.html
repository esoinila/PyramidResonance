Microphone

Microphone off

Easiest check: turn the microphone on, stop any tone, and record one sharp clap. The room rings at its own notes and no speaker is needed.

Nothing is sent anywhere. The sound of a clap is kept only if you save that measurement with "Keep the sound" ticked; everything else is analysed and discarded. On an iPhone, turning the microphone on can move the sound from a Bluetooth speaker to the phone itself. If the tone gets quiet, turn the microphone off and judge by ear, or use the clap.

Measurement

Run a sweep or record a clap with the microphone on. Gold line: measured level. Ticks along the top: the predicted modes of the room chosen in Setup (long ticks are axial).

Sweep

Glides slowly from "From Hz" to "To Hz". With the microphone on, the level is plotted in the chart above. The speaker choice corrects for the delay a Bluetooth speaker adds.

Single tones

One tone at a time. A resonance is a tone that sounds clearly louder than its neighbours. Put the speaker in a corner and stand in another corner.

Saved measurements

Nothing saved yet. After a clap or a measured sweep, "Save this measurement" stores the spectrum here with the time and your location. You can add a photo and send it by mail or to another app.

Saved on this phone only, until you send them. The location needs "Precise Location" allowed for this page. Photos are kept inside the app (reduced in size) and travel with the measurement when you send it; a web page cannot write to the phone's photo gallery itself.

Room

Model: rigid rectangular room. Real rooms have openings, sloped ceilings and people in them, so expect measured peaks within a few percent of the prediction, not on it. The curve also contains your speaker's and microphone's own response.

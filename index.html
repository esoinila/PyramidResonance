Microphone

Microphone off

Easiest check: turn the microphone on, stop any tone, and record one sharp clap. The room rings at its own notes and no speaker is needed.

Nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it. Every clap is saved on this phone with its sound, also one that is cut short. The one thing the app cannot save is a clap during whose 4 seconds the phone shuts the app down completely; a sweep in that case is recovered up to its last two seconds at the next start. On an iPhone, turning the microphone on can move the sound from a Bluetooth speaker to the phone itself. If the tone gets quiet, turn the microphone off and judge by ear, or use the clap.

Measurement

Run a sweep or record a clap with the microphone on. Gold line: measured level. Ticks along the top: the predicted modes of the room chosen in Setup (long ticks are axial).

Every measurement is saved on this phone by itself the moment it finishes, a clap with its sound. Nothing needs to be tapped. They collect on the Saved tab at the top of the page, where you can send them.

Glides slowly from "From Hz" to "To Hz" (both in Setup). With the microphone on, the level is plotted in the chart above. The speaker choice corrects for the delay a Bluetooth speaker adds.

Single tones

One tone at a time. A resonance is a tone that sounds clearly louder than its neighbours. Put the speaker in a corner and stand in another corner.

Saved measurements

Nothing saved yet. Every clap and every measured sweep is stored here by itself the moment it finishes, with the time, your location and, for a clap, its sound. You can add a photo and send it by mail or to another app.

Where the data is: inside this app's own storage on this phone, and nowhere else. It stays when the app is closed, updated or the phone restarts. It is erased if you delete the app's icon from the home screen or clear Safari's website data. Sending is the only backup: each measurement shows "not sent yet" until it has been sent.

The location needs "Precise Location" allowed for this page. Photos are kept inside the app (reduced to 1600 px) and travel with the measurement when you send it; a web page cannot write to the phone's photo gallery itself.

Room

Model: rigid rectangular room. Real rooms have openings, sloped ceilings and people in them, so expect measured peaks within a few percent of the prediction, not on it. The curve also contains your speaker's and microphone's own response.
